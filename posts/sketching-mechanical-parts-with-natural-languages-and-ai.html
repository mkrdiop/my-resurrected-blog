Sketching Mechanical Parts with Natural Languages and AI

I don't know if it's correct to talk about great people who passed away as I do but every time I watch again the past speeches of Sir clayton christensen I get the chance and opportunities to see how he has expressed many ideas that still hold and are applicable by entrepreneurs. even his most expressed theory about the revolution of minimills has still an application that impacts the industry of manufacturing and makes it accessible to a wider audience of makers than the small wealthy industrials that used to have the financial power to buy the expensive machining tools and robots that make a big factory.

Many predictions of dear sir clayton happened today as he had described them with his theory like the disruption of Venture capital by private equity. A possible disruption about which he talked about when he was on a panel with Marc Andreessen, the well-known venture capitalist founder of A16Z.

Way before the AI revolution and natural language recognition, Sir Clayton was talking about how we could replace manual works with languages and this happened slowly like how computers at the beginning took time to catch up with our hands striking our keyboard.

If you have watched the speech you may have heard Sir Clayton talks about his journey as a student willing to be a good technical writer with this example of this exercise of describing a mechanical part that is Valve in a precise way that someone could draw a picture of it by reading the description. if your mind is good at intersecting ideas you may see that sir clayton was highlighting the possibility to describe mechanical parts with natural language using AI and the drawing of a part and this idea is emphasized when you hear Sir Clayton talk about how typing and speech recognition were slow at the beginning of personal computers when you know how good this technology is right now, all of which could highlight you how possible it is now to develop a technology that could do this technical writing that was asked to sir Clayton. This is describing a mechanical part with words with the drawing of this part as input and you could classify this kind of solution in this category of AI that is CAD to Text.

You could also apply the inverse possibility of drawing mechanical parts right from their descriptions and this could be an interesting feature that could be appended to CAD software like Solidworks, AutoCAD, and Delmia. If you care about Sir Clayton's theory you acknowledge that these kinds of Cad to text and text-to-CAD solutions will not be able to draw complex mechanical parts from text or describe complex mechanical parts of industrial process drawings but with incremental improvements, these AI technologies about mechanical parts and industrial systems will be better and better and we could expect AI drawing very complex mechanical parts and plant processes right from the specification written in text.

if you find these ideas are worth pursuing then you could start right now because the amount of time that your machine learning models will need to be accurate at describing mechanical parts from CAD files or drawing them from their textual descriptions will be considerable what gives a head start to the entrepreneurs like you who will start working on these AI ideas right now.
