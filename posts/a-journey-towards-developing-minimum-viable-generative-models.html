A Journey Towards Developing Minimum Viable Generative Models

a Lesson For AI Entrepreneurs Learned from Building ZikCut

When starting a generative AI project, one of the most important considerations is the variability of your data source. This characteristic can make or break your efforts to build an initial, working model. For a video-generative AI startup like ZikCut, starting with data sources that present less variation from sequence to sequence has been a deliberate choice. It’s a decision that’s already paying off as I navigate the complexities of AI development, all while preparing for the next steps: demonstrating value to investors and scaling up.

Why Variability Matters in Generative AI

Data variability refers to the diversity and complexity of patterns present in your training dataset. For an AI entrepreneur, this is a critical factor to consider. Models are much easier to train when the data exhibits predictable, stable, and repeatable patterns. The less variability there is in your dataset, the faster you can get to a functioning prototype.

Let me explain this with an example. Training a generative AI to produce videos of two dancing balls is far simpler than generating complex scenes like the synchronized movements of millions of particles or swarms of drones. The former involves fewer variables and simpler patterns, making it easier for the model to learn and replicate the data. As the complexity of the patterns increases, so does the computational cost, training time, and expertise required to produce a viable model.

The ZikCut Approach is Starting Simple

At ZikCut, the strategy has been to focus first on video sources with stable and repeatable movements. This decision aligns with the principle of minimizing variability in the early stages. For example, the initial goal is to create a generative model that can illustrate songs with simple visuals. These might include synchronized animations, straightforward transitions, or repetitive, rhythm-driven movements. Starting with these kinds of videos enables the model to achieve accurate results faster and with less computational overhead.

By focusing on such a narrow scope, ZikCut’s development timeline benefits in two ways:

  1. Rapid Iteration: A simple dataset allows for quicker feedback cycles. You can see early results and refine your approach without waiting for weeks or months of training.
  2. Demonstrable Progress: Investors and collaborators are more likely to engage with a functioning prototype, even if it’s basic, rather than theoretical ideas or untested models.

From Minimum Viable Product to Minimum Viable Generative Model

In traditional entrepreneurship, the concept of a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) is well-known: a stripped-down version of your product that demonstrates its core functionality. For generative AI, I’ve adopted what I call a Minimum Viable Generative Model (MVGM). The focus isn’t on economic viability but on entrepreneurial testability — in other words, producing something tangible that works, even at a basic level.

An MVGM lets you explore and validate the potential of your idea. It’s a stepping stone, not the end goal. With ZikCut, the MVGM might be a model that generates simple video animations synchronized to music — a proof of concept that showcases what’s possible.

Preparing for Advanced Models

The journey doesn’t stop at creating a working MVGM. Advanced generative models require massive computing power, more complex datasets, and extended training times. However, getting to the MVGM stage puts you in a much stronger position to secure funding. Venture capitalists are more likely to invest in a project that has shown tangible results, no matter how basic, rather than one that exists only on paper.

With ZikCut, the ultimate vision involves creating generative AI capable of producing intricate, visually stunning videos with dynamic, complex objects. These might include full music videos, cinematic sequences, or animations that evolve interactively with the input of creators. But to reach that level, it’s essential to lay a solid foundation first.

Current Progress and Challenges

At ZikCut, I — as both founder and developer — have already identified and collected the initial training data. This dataset includes a carefully curated selection of videos designed to meet the criteria of low variability and high repeatability. The focus now is on obtaining the required computing power to train our first MVGMs.

The vision is broad, but the first steps are focused. Sound, picture, and video generation will be the initial outputs of our generative models. These are areas where both enterprise and consumer markets overlap significantly — after all, creating and consuming visual and auditory content is a significant part of daily life for many people.

Lessons for AI Entrepreneurs

For anyone considering launching their own generative AI startup, here are some key takeaways from ZikCut’s journey so far:

  1. Start Small: Choose a manageable scope for your first project. The simpler your initial dataset and output, the faster you’ll see results.
  2. Prioritize Testability: Focus on building a Minimum Viable Generative Model. Even basic functionality can demonstrate your concept’s potential.
  3. Optimize Data Selection: Select training data that minimizes variability and maximizes repeatable patterns. This will make training more efficient and cost-effective.
  4. Plan for Scale: While starting small is important, keep your end goal in mind. Use the MVGM as a stepping stone to secure funding and scale up your efforts.
  5. Understand Your Market: Focus on areas where your generative AI can make a meaningful impact. For ZikCut, this means concentrating on sound, picture, and video content that aligns with what both enterprises and consumers value.

The Road Ahead

The journey to building advanced generative AI models is both challenging and rewarding. For ZikCut, the next steps involve turning our initial MVGMs into polished, scalable systems that can revolutionize music video generation. The road ahead will undoubtedly include technical hurdles, funding challenges, and countless iterations. But by focusing on a structured approach — starting simple and building complexity over time — I’m confident that ZikCut will not only succeed but also inspire other AI entrepreneurs to follow a similar path.

Generative AI is still in its early days, and the possibilities are endless. If you’re thinking about diving into this field, remember: start with stable patterns, aim for testable results, and use those results to pave the way for bigger, more ambitious dreams. That’s the path we’re taking at ZikCut, and it’s one I hope will resonate with others in this exciting space.
