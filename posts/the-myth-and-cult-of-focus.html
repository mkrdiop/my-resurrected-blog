The Myth and Cult of Focus

Why Obsession with Focus is Overrated and Why We Need Defocus

Diop Papa Makhtar

·
·
Feb 28, 2025

--

Everywhere you turn, from self-help books to productivity gurus, you hear the same mantra: “Focus is the key to success.” The word itself has spread across the globe, transcending linguistic and cultural boundaries. It is no longer confined to English but has found its way into many languages, including Wolof, where the concept of “focus” is introduced as an essential mindset for personal and professional growth.

Yet, despite this global obsession with focus, we find ourselves stuck in cycles of stagnation, unable to solve some of the world’s most pressing problems. What if the solution isn’t more focus, but rather defocus? What if, instead of narrowing our vision, we expanded it? What if, instead of hyper-fixating on singular goals, we embraced curiosity, creativity, and fluid thinking?

In this article, we’ll challenge the dominant narrative that glorifies focus and explore why defocus might be the key to unlocking innovation and progress.

The Rise of Focus as a Global Buzzword

Focus has become a universal term. You hear it in business meetings, self-improvement workshops, educational institutions, and even casual conversations. It is emphasized in personal development, where people are told that the only way to achieve their goals is through relentless focus. It is prescribed in schools, where students are encouraged to concentrate on single subjects rather than explore diverse fields. It dominates corporate cultures, where employees are pressured to focus on quarterly targets without room for broader vision.

This hyper-focus mentality has been so widely embraced that it has seeped into languages across the world. Even in Wolof, a language spoken predominantly in Senegal and parts of West Africa, the word “focus” is increasingly used in its original English form or translated into equivalent expressions. This global spread of the term highlights just how deeply ingrained the idea has become.

But has this fixation on focus really delivered the results we hoped for?

The Problem with an Overemphasis on Focus

  1. Tunnel Vision Stifles Creativity
    When we focus too much on one thing, we often miss the bigger picture. Hyper-focus narrows our vision and blinds us to alternative perspectives. Some of the most groundbreaking discoveries in history happened by accident — when people weren’t focused on a specific outcome but were open to exploring various possibilities. Think Dear Isaac Newtow and the finding of the law of gravity.
  2. Focus Doesn’t Guarantee Problem-Solving
    The world’s biggest problems — climate change, inequality, global health crises — aren’t going to be solved by simply concentrating harder on existing methods. These issues require creativity, cross-disciplinary thinking, and unconventional approaches, all of which demand an openness to defocus rather than a rigid adherence to focus.
  3. Stress and Burnout
    Hyper-focus is often equated with productivity, but in reality, it can lead to stress, exhaustion, and burnout. People are pushed to work longer hours, eliminate distractions, and suppress their natural curiosity. Yet, studies have shown that taking breaks, stepping away from work, and allowing the mind to wander (synonym of defocus) leads to greater problem-solving abilities and mental well-being.
  4. Focus is a Privilege
    Not everyone has the luxury of focus. The ability to concentrate exclusively on one goal assumes that basic needs are met and that external pressures aren’t a factor. In many parts of the world, people must juggle multiple responsibilities, adapt to unpredictable circumstances, and think on their feet. For them, defocus is not a disadvantage, it is a survival skill and a tool for creativity.

Defocus: A New Paradigm for Creativity and Solutions

Instead of chasing focus as the ultimate solution, we should be embracing defocus — a broader, more fluid approach to thinking and problem-solving. Defocus doesn’t mean a lack of direction; it means opening ourselves to multiple perspectives, allowing new ideas to emerge, and fostering innovation.

What Does Defocus Look Like?

  1. Some of the most brilliant ideas emerge when we’re not actively searching for them. Think about those “aha” moments that happen in the shower, during a walk, or while daydreaming. Defocusing allows the subconscious mind to form connections that wouldn’t be possible through rigid, linear thinking. Our cognitive system is constantly working in the background — linking ideas, grouping related concepts, and creating broader contexts that can lead to new insights. If you want to dive deeper into this, I highly recommend reading some cognitive science books.
  2. Cross-Pollination of Ideas
    Creativity thrives at the intersection of disciplines. Scientists who engage with artists, entrepreneurs who explore philosophy, and engineers who study history often come up with revolutionary ideas. Instead of focusing narrowly on one area, we should encourage exploration across different fields.
  3. Embracing Playfulness and Curiosity
    Play is one of the most powerful tools for problem-solving. When we play, we experiment, take risks, and think without constraints. Many breakthroughs in science and technology have come from playful exploration rather than rigid, focused work. It’s surprising that game-based learning hasn’t been more widely popularized and adopted as a primary method of teaching — especially considering that almost everyone now has a computer on their desk or in their pocket.
  4. Thinking in Systems, Not Just Goals
    Rather than obsessing over a single target, we should view problems as part of larger interconnected systems. Defocus allows us to see how different factors influence each other, leading to more holistic and effective solutions.

Why Defocus is the Future

The world is changing faster than ever. Traditional ways of thinking — especially rigid focus — are proving inadequate for solving modern challenges. We need to cultivate an adaptive mindset, one that embraces uncertainty, welcomes diverse perspectives, and encourages flexible problem-solving.

  • Education should prioritize interdisciplinary learning over specialization. Instead of forcing students to focus on rigid curricula, we should encourage curiosity-driven learning that spans multiple subjects.
  • Workplaces should value creative exploration. Employees should have the freedom to pursue side projects, collaborate across departments, and engage in activities that spark new ideas. I wonder if Google’s famous 20% time policy for side projects has played a role in keeping it one of the biggest tech companies to this day.
  • Personal development should emphasize adaptability. Instead of trying to master focus, we should embrace flexibility, serendipity, and lifelong learning.

The future belongs to those who can see beyond what is immediately in front of them. It belongs to the defocused thinkers who can connect seemingly unrelated ideas, adapt to new situations, and come up with creative solutions that focused minds might miss.

Let’s Rethink Focus by Adopting Defocus

Focus has been sold as the ultimate solution to success and problem-solving, but the reality is more complex. While focus has its place, an overemphasis on it limits creativity, innovation, and adaptability.

Instead, we need to embrace defocus — a mindset that encourages open exploration, cross-disciplinary thinking, and holistic problem-solving. By shifting our perspective, we can unlock new ways of thinking and tackle the challenges of the 21st century with greater creativity and resilience.

So the next time someone tells you to “just focus,” consider whether what you really need is to defocus instead.
