The Inertia of Innovation

Overcoming Resistance as an Entrepreneur

Diop Papa Makhtar
·
Mar 30, 2025

--

Centuries ago, Galileo Galilei, a pioneer of modern science, articulated a profound concept — inertia. This fundamental law of physics states that bodies, left to their own devices, resist any change to their initial state. A stationary object prefers to remain still, and a moving object prefers to continue moving in a straight line, at a constant speed, unless acted upon by an external force. This simple yet powerful principle, a cornerstone of classical mechanics, offers a surprisingly insightful lens through which to examine the challenges of entrepreneurship.

Entrepreneurship, at its core, is a relentless dance with change. It’s about disrupting established norms, introducing novel ideas, and navigating uncharted territories. Yet, this very essence of innovation often collides with an equally powerful force — resistance. Just as a physical body resists alteration, so too do individuals, organizations, and even entire markets resist the new and the unfamiliar.

This inherent resistance is a recurring theme in the writings of thought leaders like Steven Pressfield and Seth Godin, who have dedicated considerable attention to understanding its nature and impact. Pressfield, in his insightful work, paints resistance as a “mighty and diabolical enemy,” a force that relentlessly seeks to thwart our creative endeavors. He acknowledges its formidable power but importantly, he also recognizes its vulnerability. Pressfield argues that resistance, like any natural force, operates according to “fixed and immutable laws.” Understanding these laws, he suggests, is the key to overcoming its grip.

Seth Godin, meanwhile, approaches resistance from a slightly different angle, focusing on its internal manifestation. He describes it as the “internal force that keeps us from doing our most important creative work.” This internal resistance manifests as procrastination, self-doubt, and a myriad of other psychological barriers that prevent us from realizing our potential. It’s the voice that whispers, “It’s too difficult,” or “It’s not worth it,” or “Someone else will do it better.”

The parallels between Galileo’s inertia and the resistance faced by entrepreneurs are striking. Just as a physical object requires an external force to overcome its inertia, entrepreneurs need to summon an internal force, a resolute determination, to overcome the resistance that stands in their way. This force might take the form of unwavering belief in their vision, a relentless pursuit of their goals, or a deep-seated passion that fuels their efforts.

The challenge, however, lies not just in recognizing resistance but in understanding its insidious nature. It’s not always a loud, aggressive opponent; it often operates subtly, disguising itself as rationalization, procrastination, or even seemingly harmless distractions. It’s the endless scrolling, the unnecessary meetings, the constant checking of emails — all tactics employed by resistance to derail our progress.

For entrepreneurs, understanding and combating resistance is not merely a matter of personal productivity; it’s a matter of survival. The marketplace is unforgiving, and those who succumb to resistance risk being left behind. To thrive, entrepreneurs must develop strategies to overcome this formidable foe.

One crucial strategy is to cultivate a deep awareness of resistance’s tactics. Recognizing its subtle manipulations is the first step towards neutralizing its power. This requires introspection, self-awareness, and a willingness to confront the uncomfortable truths about our own behavior.

Another essential strategy is to establish routines and rituals that minimize the opportunity for resistance to take hold. Creating dedicated time for focused work, eliminating distractions, and setting clear goals can help to create an environment where creativity and productivity can flourish.

Furthermore, building a supportive network of mentors, peers, and advisors can provide invaluable encouragement and guidance. Sharing experiences, receiving feedback, and celebrating small victories can help to reinforce our resolve and keep us on track.

Overcoming resistance is not a one-time battle; it’s an ongoing process. It requires constant vigilance, unwavering commitment, and a willingness to learn from our setbacks. But the rewards are immeasurable. By confronting and conquering resistance, entrepreneurs unlock their creative potential, transform their visions into reality, and contribute to the ongoing evolution of the world around them.

Galileo’s law of inertia reminds us that change is inherently challenging. But it also reminds us that change is possible. With the right force, the right determination, and the right strategies, entrepreneurs can overcome the inertia of resistance and create a future that is both innovative and impactful.
