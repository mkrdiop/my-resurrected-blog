Targeting a Market of 65,000 Writers and 54,000 Students

The narrative of Silicon Valley often tells a tale of exponential growth, of startups like Facebook and OpenAI scaling to millions, even billions, of users. We’re told to “go big or go home,” to build solutions for a global audience from day one. It’s an alluring myth, one that many aspiring entrepreneurs, including myself, have chased. Yet, I’ve learned the hard way that the true path to success often lies in a much simpler, more focused direction: the Minimum Viable Audience (MVA).

My journey began with a vision of conquering the vast and crowded market of developer tools. I poured my energy into a promising idea called SwapJS, a tool designed to serve millions of developers worldwide. The potential was immense, but the competition was fierce. Every corner of that market is saturated with established players and innovative newcomers, all vying for the attention of the same audience. The noise was deafening, and my initial attempts to break through felt like shouting into a void.
