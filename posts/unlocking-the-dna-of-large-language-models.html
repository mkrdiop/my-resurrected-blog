How Evolutionary Biology Can Inform the Next Generation of AI

The parallels between the high-dimensional vector space of Large Language Model (LLM) embeddings and the complex genetic information encoded in DNA offer a fascinating new perspective on artificial intelligence. While seemingly disparate, both systems represent a form of highly compressed information that governs an entity’s behavior and potential, hinting at a potential new research field at the intersection of computational linguistics and evolutionary biology.

The Genetic Blueprint of Language

At its core, a large language model operates by converting text into a numerical format it can understand and manipulate. This conversion process is driven by embeddings, which are high-dimensional vectors representing the semantic meaning of words, phrases, or even entire documents. Unlike a simple word-for-word translation, these embeddings are a dense, contextualized representation. For example, the embedding for “bank” would be different in “river bank” versus “savings bank” because the model has learned to capture the nuances of context.
