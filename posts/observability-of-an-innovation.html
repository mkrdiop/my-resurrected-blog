Observability of an innovation

The case of Uber and its network of independent drivers

Diop Makhtar

Just now·3 min read

The observability of a new idea, call it innovation if you want can be carried by one or more other new or old ideas. if you take the example of Uber, it is an idea lacking observability in the real and physical world unlike cellular phones because you can clearly see a cellular phone when someone uses it or hear it when it rings near you at this restaurant where you are having lunch. The cellular phone is a very real-world observable innovation and this observability participated in its rapid rate of adoption as highlighted by rogers and studied by other scientists. Nokia the finish company has benefited from this characteristical observability of cellular phones but uber with its network of independent drivers this observability is missing, unlike taxis that are very real-world observable with a clear distinctive image related to the country or the city they belong to. Like this taxi from Dakar, Senegal

But we can state that for uber the observability of its innovation is deported, I mean carried by every taxi you will see given that you already know what Uber is and what it stands for. Here the deported observability is about rehearsing awareness and this is something that plays a major role in almost all industries making them duopolistic by nature. You can see that this theory of duopolistic industry that I am trying to highlight to you is not a new idea because almost a year ago I was writing about it and how it shaped the personal computer operating system industry (here). Duopolistic because every time an idea X is observable it opens the opportunity for another idea Y to be also Observable by using Y is like X but … as a distinctive positioning advantage. If the innovator or marketer that comes up with the distinctive idea Y do his or her job well then every time X will be observable physically then there will be a high propensity for Y to be cognitively observable mostly because that’s how our brain is wired and it is how it works. You may have already discovered in my past articles about cognitive science that our brain is wired to extremely use similarities and symmetries and it very often match patterns. Y is like X but … is a very powerful marketing tool is what I have learned From these three foundational books, I mean Tribe, This is marketing and the Practice and it is not about copying but playing with the edges and choosing to be specific not average. It is about choosing the smallest set of people who can’t live without your innovation and who have all means to not only see your innovation but also help others see it. The Observability of one innovation can be engineered to make it spread like a virus and that’s what I am trying to achieve with all these tech-enabled ideas.
