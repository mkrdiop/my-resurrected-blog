The Power of Improving Existing Systems

Disruptive and sustainable innovation are not mutually exclusive

Diop Papa Makhtar

·
·
4 hours ago

--

Innovation is often perceived as the process of creating something entirely new, something groundbreaking that disrupts industries and redefines the way we interact with the world. However, an equally important and often overlooked aspect of innovation is improving existing systems — taking what already works and making it better, more efficient, and more adaptable to modern needs. This balance between sustaining and disruptive innovation is crucial in shaping progress across industries.

A Personal Journey into Sustainable Innovation

My journey into engineering and technology began with a project I undertook during my undergraduate studies at Senelec, the national electric company of Senegal. My task was to enhance the instrumentation of three trunk generator units that operated using both heavy and light fuel. These units had been deployed at a time when digital technology was not yet widely used, meaning that most of the supervision and control processes were still being handled manually.

My goal was clear: I needed to understand the circuits involved in the supervision and control of these units and design a new system that leveraged digital technology to improve efficiency and reliability. It was a fascinating challenge that introduced me to software like LabVIEW and allowed me to apply automation concepts I had learned at university.

More importantly, however, this project was my first real exposure to the essence of my professional mission — taking existing systems and making them better. While working on this project, I realized that improving what already exists can be just as impactful as creating something entirely new. This realization has since guided my career and approach to problem-solving.

The Intersection of Sustainable and Disruptive Innovation

In discussions about innovation, we often hear about disruptive innovation — the kind that changes industries overnight. Companies like Google, Apple, and Tesla have become synonymous with disruptive innovation, introducing new categories of products and services that redefine entire markets. However, what is often overlooked is that even these companies thrive not just on disruption but also on sustainable innovation — incremental improvements that enhance existing technologies.

Take Apple, for example. While the company introduced the iPod, iPhone, and iPad as disruptive innovations, much of its success lies in continuously refining and improving its existing products. Each new iPhone is not a complete reinvention but an improvement on the last, integrating better hardware, software, and user experience. This sustained innovation has allowed Apple to maintain its dominance in the market.

Similarly, Google’s search engine was a disruptive innovation when it was first introduced, outperforming competitors with its superior algorithm. However, its continued success is largely due to sustainable innovation — constant refinements in search algorithms, AI integrations, and personalized experiences that keep Google ahead of the competition.

Why Sustainable Innovation is a Shortcut to Progress

The idea of improving existing systems is not just a corporate strategy; it is also an efficient approach for entrepreneurs, engineers, and innovators looking to make an impact. As marketing expert Seth Godin pointed out in his blog post The Lonely Unicorn, if you’re looking for an interesting project, one of the most effective shortcuts is to find something that has already been done and then improve upon it.

He writes: “If you’re looking for an interesting project, a useful shortcut is finding someone who has already done something similar and then altering or improving it. No extra points for being the one and only one in your category.”

This insight is powerful because it emphasizes that you don’t need to reinvent the wheel to make a meaningful contribution. Instead of spending years trying to create something entirely novel, innovators can often achieve more immediate impact by refining and enhancing what already exists.

The Strategy of Owning and Leading a Category

Of course, while sustainable innovation is a valuable approach, there is also merit in striving to create and lead a new category. Some of the most successful companies and entrepreneurs have managed to do both — start by improving an existing system and, in the process, carve out a new category for themselves.

For instance, before Apple revolutionized the smartphone industry, it entered the MP3 player market with the iPod, refining digital music consumption before transitioning into mobile phones. Similarly, Google initially built a better search engine, which ultimately positioned it as the leader in the search category. These companies didn’t just create something new out of nowhere; they studied existing models, improved them, and eventually defined entirely new markets.

Applying the Principle of Sustainable Innovation

If you are an aspiring entrepreneur, engineer, or innovator, embracing sustainable innovation can be an effective strategy to build momentum in your career or business. Here are some practical steps to apply this principle:

  1. Identify Existing Systems with Gaps — Look for industries, products, or technologies that have room for improvement. This could be anything from outdated software to inefficient business processes.
  2. Study the Current Landscape — Understand the existing solutions, their strengths, and their weaknesses. Learn from what has already been done rather than starting from scratch.
  3. Incorporate Modern Technology — Many older systems can be vastly improved by integrating digital technologies, automation, AI, or IoT solutions. As seen in my Senelec project, leveraging digital tools can enhance efficiency and effectiveness.
  4. Iterate and Improve Continuously — Sustainable innovation is an ongoing process. Make small but meaningful changes, test them, and continue refining your solution over time.
  5. Look for Opportunities to Define a New Category — While improving existing systems, pay attention to emerging trends and customer needs. Sometimes, making something better leads to the realization that an entirely new category can be created.

Conclusion

Innovation does not always have to be about creating something from scratch. Sustainable innovation — taking what exists and improving it — is just as valuable and often more accessible for those looking to make an impact. Whether in engineering, business, or any other field, understanding how to refine and enhance existing solutions is a powerful skill that drives progress.

Disruptive and sustainable innovation are not mutually exclusive; in fact, they work best when applied together. By improving what is already out there, you not only contribute to current advancements but also set the foundation for entirely new breakthroughs. So, whether you are an engineer working on automation, an entrepreneur refining a business model, or a creative professional enhancing user experiences, remember: sometimes, the best way forward is not to start from zero but to build upon what already exists.
