The knowledge economic growth theory

Knowledge creates economic growth, which spurs knowledge, which begets growth. That’s a viral growth cycle and the most important.

Diop Makhtar

Just now·2 min read

For a long time, we thought that economic growth was driven by population and machinery through industrialization but it turns out the big factor of economic growth is not simply but hardly knowledge.

New growth theory, built on work done in the 1950s by Nobel laureate Robert Solow and championed by Stanford University’s Paul Romer, points out that knowledge, particularly technology, is crucial to economic growth.

More knowledge leads to more ideas that bring more and more knowledge and the cycle of knowledge continues this back and forth from knowledge to ideas. Ideas being the starting point of almost all innovative products and services like those like the internet that transformed our world and made it richer and richer. Of course with wealth inequalities issues but you can guess that knowledge is at the core of economic growth.

Then is important for us to know and care about the containers, medium, and drivers of knowledge. Education was designed as a driver of knowledge but it seems that the industrial complex transformed it from a knowledge promoting system to a plant producing cheaper workers and managers rather than incubators of knowledge and ideas. But this is not a miss of education managers or professors and searchers but simply every one of us. We often engaged ourselves or our kids into the education system by luring this future job that we could and would have in this big bank or top administration making schools and universities the tool of successful ventures.

Books are knowledge containers that bring new ideas into the world and it is time for them to disrupt the education system that uses them as tools while they are more than that. Books as a professional currency, Books as a medium of success, books that connect people with the same goal and help them achieve. Data-driven books by people who believe in the power of knowledge as a key factor of growth.
