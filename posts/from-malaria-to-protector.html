From Malaria to Protector

Repurposing Insects like Mosquitoes for Global Health and Agriculture

Diop Papa Makhtar
·
Aug 22, 2025

--

Malaria, a disease that continues to cast a long shadow over global health, has long been a formidable adversary, particularly in Africa, where it claims hundreds of thousands of lives each year and stifles economic growth. Despite incredible progress fueled by organizations like the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, the battle against this ancient scourge rages on. The tireless efforts to develop new drugs, distribute bed nets, and create vaccines have been monumental, yet the sheer cunning of the Anopheles mosquito, the primary vector, makes eradication a seemingly impossible goal.

Thinking about the relentless spread of malaria, it occurred to me that the very mechanism that makes this disease so devastating — transmission via a mosquito’s bite — might also be its undoing. What if nature’s most infamous pest could be reprogrammed and repurposed? The very same species that transmits the parasite could one day become a tool for mass immunization and crop protection.
