A New Physics Engine for Social Worlds

Forget gravity. The real game-changer isn’t how things fall, but how people interact.

Diop Papa Makhtar
·
5 days ago

--

I’ve been wrestling with an idea that feels more like a revelation than a simple concept. For years, we’ve watched game worlds grow in visual fidelity and scale, but a persistent hollowness has always lingered beneath the surface. We’ve built stunning, sprawling cities filled with beautifully rendered non-player characters, yet they feel like intricate puppets on a stage, their actions predetermined, their lives lacking true spontaneity. The illusion, no matter how convincing, eventually cracks.

What if we could change that? What if we could imbue these digital worlds with the messy, unpredictable, and emergent complexity of real life? That’s the question that led me to the idea of a Social Physics Engine, a concept I’ve been calling SoPhysics.

For too long, the “physics engine” in a game has meant gravity, collisions, and momentum. It’s about how a car crumples on impact or how a crate tumbles down a hill. But the real, captivating physics of a world isn’t just about objects; it’s about people. It’s about social dynamics.
